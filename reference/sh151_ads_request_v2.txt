Subject: SH 151 LiDAR — ground control list and adjustment report

Hi [name],

I've finished my independent accuracy checks on the SH 151 corridor
LiDAR (Flights 1-3, the delivered strip files labeled
`..._LCP2_s.las`). I have a clearer picture than when I first wrote,
and one finding I think you'll want to see.

Good news first, unchanged: the flight data itself looks excellent.
Where flight lines overlap — including the western corridor flown
twice, about five hours apart — the strips agree with each other to
within about a hundredth of a foot. I have no concerns about the
sensor calibration or about how the flight lines fit together.

What I'm seeing: at specific spots along the corridor the delivered
surface sits consistently higher or lower than my ground survey, by
up to about 0.23 ft on hard pavement, and every strip from every
flight reads the same offset at the same spot. The differences follow
locations on the ground, not flight lines.

The new part is that I have a second, independent measurement of
those same points. The stereo photogrammetry from this project was
measured against the same survey marks, and at 14 of the 16 marks
where the LiDAR is out by more than a tenth of a foot, the imagery
lands within about a tenth of a foot of the surveyed elevation — with
no systematic vertical bias across the 65 marks as a whole. Where the
LiDAR reads clean, the imagery agrees too. So two independent sensors
sit over one control network and only one of them disagrees with the
ground survey. That points at the delivered surface rather than at
the marks.

A few hard-surface examples, all on ground flat enough that grade and
vegetation are not in play:

- point 200203, E 2,488,656 / N 424,180: surface reads 0.23 ft high;
  imagery is within 0.02 ft of my elevation
- point 200205, E 2,487,137 / N 425,265: 0.22 ft high; imagery within
  0.02 ft
- point 80809, E 2,476,021 / N 416,011: 0.15 ft low; imagery within
  0.01 ft
- point 80818, E 2,487,585 / N 424,919: 0.14 ft high; imagery within
  0.01 ft

In fairness, the same test flagged two of my own marks — 80806 and
80822 — where both the LiDAR and the imagery disagree with my
published elevation. Those are mine to re-observe and I've pulled
them out of the comparison. I mention them so you know the check cuts
both ways.

Separately, there is a broad east-west tilt in the comparison of
about 0.14 ft per mile across the corridor, on top of the local
differences. I want to be straight that my imagery check cannot
resolve that part — the aerotriangulation is adjusted to these same
marks, so it would absorb a tilt in my network rather than reveal
one. A difference in geoid model between your processing and my
survey would produce exactly this, which is why item 3 below matters
more than it looks.

Since the file names indicate the strips were fitted to LiDAR control
("LCP2"), my working conclusion is that the values used in that
adjustment disagree with my survey at these locations, which would
pull every strip the same direction at each spot. To confirm that or
rule it out, could you send:

1. The LiDAR control point list used for the LCP2 adjustment — point
   numbers, coordinates, elevations, and where those values came from
   (who surveyed them, when, and on what datum and geoid).
2. The strip adjustment / calibration report from processing — the
   before-and-after fit to those control points, and what corrections
   were applied to the strips.
3. The geoid model used to get to NAVD88. My control, checkpoints and
   OSSDA shots were all observed by Frontier Land Survey in Oklahoma
   State Plane North on GEOID18; if your processing used a different
   model, that would explain the corridor tilt and is worth settling
   before anything else.
4. The unadjusted strips (before the LCP2 fit), or word on whether
   they can be regenerated. The raw flight data looks strong enough
   that a clean re-fit — or possibly just a bias shift — should
   resolve everything.

Happy to send my check-point comparisons, the per-mark numbers, and
the imagery residuals in whatever format is useful. The flight work
looks good; I just need to see what the surface was fitted to.
